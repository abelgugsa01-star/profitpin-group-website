## Post 1 — ProfitPin (audience: drivers, incl. the pending connection invites)

The offer pops up. $14, nine miles. You've got about ten seconds.

You already know the questions. Where does it drop me? Is that a dead zone at this hour? How far am I driving back with nobody in the car? And the one that stings — is a better offer about to land on the other app while I'm sitting here deciding?

Nobody answers all that in ten seconds. So you go with your gut and take it. Then around midnight you add up the day, and it doesn't come out the way it felt.

That's not a you problem. Dispatching is a whole job — fleets pay someone to do it full time. Drive for yourself and you're expected to do it in the time it takes to read a notification.

That's what I built. ProfitPin is personal dispatch for a one-person operation. It watches your apps at once — Uber, Lyft, Roadie, Instacart and more — and gives you one answer on each offer: take, watch, or skip. Your car and your costs in the math, not an average of somebody else's.

It's in early trial on Android. I'd rather have ten drivers using it hard and telling me what's wrong with it than a thousand downloads and silence.

If you drive full time, comment or message me and I'll get you in.

## Post 2 — the four desktop products (audience: owners, office managers, bookkeepers)

It's 9pm and the spreadsheet is still open.

Everyone's gone home. There's one number that won't tie out, and you know that tomorrow there'll be another one, because this is the last hour of a job the software was supposed to finish.

That's the thing I keep running into. There's no shortage of software in these industries — the systems are good. They do 90% of the work and then stop, and the last 10% quietly becomes somebody's evening. Usually the office manager's. Often the owner's.

Four of them I've now built for:

**Auction houses.** You're holding other people's money in trust. Every month the bank, your books and what you owe consignors all have to agree, and when they don't you have to find out why — not make it balance. SaleLedger checks the three against each other and names every difference out loud.

**Tobacco manufacturers and importers.** TTB and FDA, every month, and the numbers have to match each other. LeafLedger drafts them from your own records with every figure traced back to the row it came from.

**Small federal contractors.** You have to be able to show you haven't subcontracted past the limit — usually to someone asking on short notice. SubLimit does that calculation from your payroll and subcontractor exports and drafts the workforce report with it.

**Shops on ServiceTitan.** Supplier prices move, someone retypes a part, and six months later the pricebook has three versions of the same fitting. StockStart reads each invoice against your pricebook and flags what's new, duplicated or mispriced before it gets in.

Three rules in all four: it runs on your own computer, your records never leave it, and it prepares — you review, you sign, you file. When something doesn't add up it tells you what and where, instead of quietly balancing the month for you.

All four are available now at profitpingroup.com, first 30 days free. There are free calculators and printable month-end checklists on the sites too, no sign-up, if you just want the checklist.

And if one of these is your 9pm, I'd honestly like to know how you handle it now.
